Interdisciplinary AI: starting with questions from other fields

GET TO KNOW THE FIELD / Interdisciplinary AI

Interdisciplinary AI starts with a disciplinary question: a molecule's properties, atomic motion, or a protein's structure. Models can approximate expensive calculations, predict quantities that are difficult to measure, and shortlist candidates for experiments. The discipline still supplies the question and the standards of verification.

Three molecular examples illustrate different roles: graph networks relate structure to properties, learned potentials accelerate simulation, and AlphaFold predicts three-dimensional structures. They require different data and constraints. Understand the target quantity, label source, and test conditions before choosing a model.

What it can help with
  • Predict properties from molecular or material structure to help shortlist candidates.
  • Approximate expensive energy and force calculations for molecular dynamics.
  • Predict protein structures from sequences and related information, providing hypotheses for further analysis and experiments.

A model score answers a defined prediction question. A disciplinary conclusion also needs scrutiny of data sources, operating conditions, and independent validation.

How the ideas developed

  1. 2017

    Put disciplinary structure into representations

    MPNN unifies message-passing approaches to molecular property prediction, representing atoms and their relationships.

    MPNN · Molecular graphs ↗
  2. 2018

    Accelerate a computational process

    Deep Potential learns energies and forces from first-principles data and enters the molecular-dynamics calculation loop.

    Deep Potential · Faster simulation ↗
  3. 2021

    Build a system around a specific hard problem

    AlphaFold2 combines sequences, evolutionary information, and geometry for structure prediction.

    AlphaFold2 · Protein structure ↗

A few terms to recognize

Inductive bias
An assumption built into model design, such as a predicted molecular property remaining unchanged after rotation.
Message passing
Graph nodes receive information from neighboring nodes and edges, then update their representations.
Surrogate model
A cheaper approximation to an expensive computation. Its errors must be checked in the subsequent simulation or decision.
Out-of-distribution evaluation
Testing conditions unlike training, such as new molecular scaffolds or temperature ranges, to assess the intended use.

A few papers to start with

Start with the paper closest to your discipline. Trace inputs, targets, data sources, and validation; use the other two to compare the roles models can play.

2017 · ICML

Neural Message Passing for Quantum Chemistry

Original paper ↗

The problem

How can atoms and their relationships predict molecular properties?

The key idea

Update node representations through messages, then aggregate them for a molecular prediction.

Why this paper

It grounds graph learning in a concrete chemistry task.

Where to start

Start with message passing, readout, targets, and data.

2018 · Physical Review Letters

Deep Potential Molecular Dynamics: A Scalable Model with the Accuracy of Quantum Mechanics

Original paper ↗Project / code ↗

The problem

How can accurate energy and force calculations become cheaper?

The key idea

Learn local atomic contributions from first-principles data while preserving problem symmetries.

Why this paper

An example of replacing an expensive step in a scientific computation.

Where to start

Trace energies and forces into simulation, then inspect physical comparisons.

A question to keep asking

Accuracy depends on covered configurations and reference calculations; new conditions need testing.

2021 · Nature

Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold

Original paper ↗Project / code ↗

The problem

The key idea

Combine evolutionary sequence information and residue relationships, iteratively refining representations and structure.

Why this paper

It connects data, geometry, and system design around a disciplinary problem.

Where to start

Hands-on paths and further reading

What interests you most may already lie outside AI: molecular properties, medical images, materials, transport, or social networks. Start with those questions, then ask where models can help.

Begin with tasks and data

For molecular questions, explore DeepChem tutorials and Therapeutics Data Commons task definitions. Ask where inputs and labels originate and how training and test sets are split.

For medical imaging, a segmentation example in MONAI Tutorials connects images, annotations, and evaluation.

For materials or molecular dynamics, read the background of DeePMD-kit, then choose your depth using disciplinary knowledge. The AI for Science index offers further leads.

Graphs and time series have their own questions

For data naturally expressed as nodes and relationships, see CS224W. For change over time, use Forecasting: Principles and Practice to understand trends, predictions, and evaluation. Its code uses R; for a Python project, see tsai.

Establish a simple reference before judging what a complex method adds. Subject knowledge helps expose conditions easily missed in the data and task.

Automated research is itself a question to investigate

Inspect Sakana AI Scientist and autoresearch: what can the system modify, how does it select experiments, and how does it evaluate results? Understand each project's scope before considering expansion to other research.

For pursuing an interdisciplinary project, return to research questions, collaboration, and experiments.

Find tasks by discipline

The AI4X catalog covers drug discovery, medical imaging, molecular dynamics, and more. Inspect data and evaluation before deciding which model knowledge to learn.
