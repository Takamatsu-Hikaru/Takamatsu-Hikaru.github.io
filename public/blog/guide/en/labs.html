Getting to know research groups

Lab pages help you see who works on a question and how the work develops over time. Pick a project and move between its paper, code, and author pages instead of memorizing lab names.

Vision, robotics, and embodied AI

  • Stanford Vision and Learning Lab, SVL: visual understanding and learning, including BEHAVIOR and ObjectFolder. Start with the robot task, then inspect data, perception, and evaluation.
  • Berkeley RAIL: learning algorithms, robotics, and vision, focused on acquiring flexible behavior. Choose a project under Publications, then find its implementation under Software.
  • Tsinghua Embodied Intelligence Lab: an official university introduction. Follow it toward teams and projects, alongside the embodied AI materials.
  • Shanghai Jiao Tong MVIG: machine vision and intelligence, with research and application information. Follow embodied projects to individual researchers.
  • Berkeley AI Research, BAIR: a broader community. Read researchers' explanations in the blog archive, then find the relevant group.

Language models and efficient computation

  • Stanford NLP: connect questions through papers, members, and teaching materials.
  • MIT HAN Lab: efficient AI, compression, deployment, and the interaction of algorithms and systems. Follow work such as AWQ and SmoothQuant for quantization and inference speed.
  • THUDM / THUKEG open-source organization: repositories leading to models, evaluation, and papers.

Industry: original releases and technical material

OpenAI News, Anthropic Research, Google DeepMind publications, and Google Research let you trace a news item to the original work. For relevant topics, inspect tasks, data, evaluation, and limitations.

After visiting a lab page

Choose a paper whose question interests you and find earlier or later work. Look at the people behind it: student pages often contain blogs, project records, or code with details the paper leaves out.

Before reaching out, organize questions and attempts using contact and collaboration. Read current recruitment conditions: for example, SVL's application entry specifies Stanford students, and MVIG's undergraduate internship entry includes institutional eligibility conditions.
